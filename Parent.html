Welcome Parents! Guide to Helping Your Child Learn

Hello Parents!

Your child’s learning journey is exciting, and you play a key role in supporting it. Here’s a detailed guide on what children aged 10 and below should be learning, how you can guide them, and activities/assignments to strengthen their learning at home.

Language & Literacy

Goal: Help children read, write, and communicate effectively.

What Children Learn

  • Alphabets: Recognize uppercase and lowercase letters
  • Phonics: Understand the sound each letter makes.
  • Reading: Comprehension and reading aloud.
  • Writing: Tracing letters, writing words, and simple sentences.

Parents Guidance on Storytelling & Listening, Retelling stories, expressing thoughts

Read together daily — even 10 minutes counts!

Point to letters and ask your child to say the sounds.

Ask questions about stories: “Who is the main character?” “What happened first?”

Assignments for Home

Letter Hunt: Find and write 5 letters from books, signs, or toys.

Match: Pick 5 new words each week, draw pictures for them, and say the word aloud.

Story Retelling: After reading a short story, ask your child to retell it in their own words.

2. Math & Numbers

Goal: Develop counting, logical thinking, and problem-solving skills.
What Children Learn
  • Counting Numbers: Recognize numbers 1–100.
  • Basic Operations: Addition, subtraction, and simple multiplication.
  • Shapes & Geometry: Identify 2D and 3D shapes.
  • Measurement & Time: Lengths, weights, volumes, and telling time.
  • Patterns & Sequences: Recognize and create repeating patterns.

Parent Guidance

Use everyday objects (fruits, toys) to teach counting and simple sums.

Ask your child to identify shapes in your home.

Introduce simple schedules to teach time (morning, noon, evening).

Assignments for Home

Counting Game: Count how many chairs, spoons, or toys are in a room.

Addition/Subtraction: Give your child 5 candies, eat 2, ask how many are left.

Shape Hunt: Find 3 objects that are squares, 3 circles, 2 triangles.

Pattern Making: Create color or bead patterns and ask your child to continue them.
